Technical Notice

Horological Timing & Mechanical Watch Disclaimer

Effective Date: January 1, 2026 • AuraDialVale Manufacture Standards

1. Nature of Mechanical Horology vs. Atomic Timekeeping

All timepieces manufactured by AuraDialVale are purely mechanical instruments powered by coiled mainsprings, oscillating balance wheels, and escapement gear trains. Unlike quartz crystals or atomic satellite-synchronized digital clocks, mechanical movements are living kinematic systems subject to minute physical rate fluctuations influenced by wearer activity levels, gravitational orientation, atmospheric pressure, and ambient temperatures.

2. Timing Precision and Daily Rate Tolerances

Our in-house calibres are regulated to chronometer standards of -2 to +2 seconds per day under controlled laboratory conditions across 5 spatial positions. However, actual real-world timing performance will vary slightly based on individual wearing habits, daily wrist motion kinetic charging, and mainspring torque state. Timing tolerances within certified COSC parameters (-4 to +6 s/d) represent flawless mechanical operation and do not constitute a defect.

3. Water Resistance Guidelines and Gasket Maintenance

Water resistance ratings (e.g., 30m / 3 ATM for dress watches, 100m / 10 ATM for sports chronographs) refer to static laboratory pressure tests and do not imply indefinite immersion capability. Water resistance is maintained via synthetic rubber O-ring gaskets that degrade naturally over time due to sweat, chlorine, and temperature changes. Winding crowns must always be fully pushed in or screwed down before exposure to moisture. Dials and movements are not covered under warranty if moisture enters through an open crown.

4. Magnetic Field Exposure and Non-Silicon Calibres

While our silicon hairspring calibres are immune to magnetic fields up to 15,000 Gauss, classical Nivarox-sprung movements can become magnetized when placed near high-output speakers, magnetic laptop closures, or medical scanning equipment. Magnetization causes rapid rate acceleration (gaining minutes per day), which is readily resolved through simple demagnetization and does not indicate mechanical failure.
