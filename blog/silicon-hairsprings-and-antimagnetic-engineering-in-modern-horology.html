1. The Invisible Enemy: Magnetism in the Modern World

In the 18th and 19th centuries, the primary environmental threats to mechanical watch accuracy were temperature fluctuations, humidity, and physical shocks.

In the 21st century, however, mechanical watches exist in an environment saturated with invisible electromagnetic fields. Neodymium magnetic laptop clasps, smartphone speakers, tablet folio cases, induction cooktops, and MRI medical equipment generate magnetic fields ranging from 50 Gauss to over 15,000 Gauss.

According to Swiss watch repair statistics, **magnetism is the single leading cause (over 65%) of sudden timing rate failure in modern luxury mechanical timepieces**.

2. How Magnetic Fields Paralyze Traditional Nivarox Balance Springs

Traditional balance hairsprings are manufactured from iron-nickel-chromium alloys (such as Nivarox or Glucydur). While engineered to resist subtle temperature shifts, these alloys remain inherently ferromagnetic.

When exposed to a magnetic field as weak as 60 Gauss (such as resting a watch on top of a tablet case), the microscopic coils of the metallic hairspring become magnetized:

  • Adjacent active coils of the spring stick together magnetically.
  • This effectively shortens the active breathing length of the hairspring by 30% to 50%.
  • The watch immediately begins gaining **15 to 45 minutes per day**, or stops oscillating entirely.

3. Historic Antimagnetic Solutions: The Soft Iron Faraday Cage

In the mid-20th century, watchmakers protected tool watches (such as the IWC Ingenieur or Rolex Milgauss) by enclosing the movement inside a **Soft Iron Inner Case** (a Faraday cage).

The soft iron channeled magnetic flux lines around the movement rather than through it. However, soft iron cages added significant thickness (2â€“3mm), blocked the view of decorated movements through sapphire exhibition casebacks, and saturated at around 1,000 Gauss.

4. The Silicon (Silinvar) Revolution: Monocrystalline Physics

In the early 2000s, a joint research consortium between CSEM (Centre Suisse dâ€™Ã‰lectronique et de Microtechnique) and leading Haute Horlogerie manufactures developed the ultimate solution: **Monocrystalline Silicon Hairsprings** (marketed as *Silinvar* or *Spiromax*).

Silicon is an elemental semiconductor (\(\text{Si}\)). It contains zero iron, nickel, or cobalt, making it **100% non-ferrous, non-conductive, and utterly immune to magnetic fields exceeding 15,000 Gauss**. A watch equipped with a silicon hairspring can be placed directly on a high-power magnetic medical resonance device with zero change in rate.

5. Deep Reactive-Ion Etching (DRIE) Precision Manufacturing

Traditional metal hairsprings require laborious manual rolling, coiling, and hand-pinning into the collet, resulting in subtle geometrical variances between individual springs.

Silicon hairsprings are manufactured using semiconductor **Deep Reactive-Ion Etching (DRIE)** on pure silicon wafers in cleanrooms:

* Plasma gas cuts the silicon with sub-micron precision (tolerance under \(0.1\,\mu\text{m}\)).
* The collet, terminal curve, and Breguet overcoil are etched as a single, seamless monolithic component, completely eliminating attachment stress points.

To achieve perfect chronometer thermal compensation, our silicon hairsprings are heated in oxygen kilns to grow a micro-thin layer of **Silicon Dioxide (\(\text{SiO}_2\))** across the entire outer surface. Because silicon dioxide has a positive thermo-elastic coefficient, the two layers cancel each other out perfectly, maintaining an identical oscillation rate across temperatures from -10Â°C to +60Â°C.

7. Shock Resistance and Elastic Memory of Silicon Springs

Silicon has exceptional elastic memory: when deformed by a severe drop or physical shock, it snaps back instantly to its exact original geometry without permanent plastic deformation. Furthermore, silicon is one-third the density of steel, reducing the gravitational weight of the hairspring and eliminating centrifugal distortion.

In our atelier, traditional watches are diagnosed using electronic compass magnetometers. Magnetized timepieces are treated on high-frequency electronic demagnetizers that emit alternating decaying magnetic fields. For our silicon calibres, however, demagnetization is rendered completely obsolete.

10. Atomic Lattice Purity and Zero Hysteresis Loss

Metallic alloys are polycrystalline structures made of microscopic grain boundaries. Over millions of repetitive flexing cycles, these metallic grains slide against each other microscopically, causing internal friction and mechanical hysteresis loss.

Monocrystalline silicon, by contrast, is a single continuous atomic crystal lattice of silicon atoms. It possesses zero grain boundaries and virtually zero internal friction. It can oscillate billions of times over decades with zero structural fatigue, zero elastic degradation, and zero change in its resonant frequency.

11. Silicon Escape Wheels and Pallet Forks (Zero Lubrication)

Beyond the hairspring, AuraDialVale fabricates the escape wheel and pallet fork from oxidized silicon coated with diamond-like carbon (DLC).

Because silicon-on-silicon contact possesses an ultra-low coefficient of friction (\(\mu < 0.05\)), the entire escapement operates **100% dry with zero liquid oil lubrication**. This eliminates the primary cause of watch aging, ensuring our movements maintain pristine amplitude and rate stability indefinitely.

12. Micro-Electromechanical Systems (MEMS) Integration

The application of MEMS technology to Haute Horlogerie allows our master watchmakers to design complex balance geometry previously impossible through conventional CNC milling.

Silicon balance wheels can feature internal aerodynamic damping slots and integrated inertia adjustment cams directly etched into the silicon disc. This seamless monolithic construction eliminates attachment screws and balance staff runout, achieving perfect dynamic balance and eliminating poise errors across all spatial planes.
